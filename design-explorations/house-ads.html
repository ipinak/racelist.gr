What works

Uses the RaceItem grid, so it costs almost nothing to build and sits naturally in the race-list scan. The orange rule and “Χορηγούμενο” column mark it clearly.

What risks

Neither product is running-related, so a row that looks like a race but isn’t one can feel like bait and erode trust in the list.

Pick this if

you want the highest click-through with minimal new UI, and you’ll cap it at one ad per ~10 rows.

What works

It turns the relevance problem into the story: you make the free site, and these are your other projects. Both products fit in one slot, and it reuses the dark tools-section style from the homepage.

What risks

It’s tall, so it pushes races down. Fine as a single mid-list or end-of-article block, but too heavy for every page.

Pick this if

you want ads that build goodwill rather than chase clicks, and can live with one placement per page.

What works

It has the strongest conversion cues for an iOS install: a real App Store badge, a rating, a device preview, and a QR fallback for desktop visitors. The banner mirrors iOS Smart App Banner conventions.

What risks

It’s visually louder than anything else on the site and needs real screenshots and ratings, or it looks empty. On a data-dense page it may come across as an intrusion.

Pick this if

installs are the goal and you have good assets (icon, screenshots, rating) to show off.

What works

It’s barely there. It uses the same JetBrains Mono “… →” language as the existing section links, so it feels native without pretending to be a race. The dismissible sticky bar remembers the dismissal.

What risks

It may be too subtle to move numbers, and it gives no room to explain what an unrelated product is.

Pick this if

protecting the reading and calendar experience matters more than ad performance.
