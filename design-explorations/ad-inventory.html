What works

This is native to racelist and the most obviously useful format: the paid race is still a real race in the right place. It needs no new creative, since it's built from the race record. The orange edge and star label keep it clearly marked.

What risks

Organizers have small budgets and buy close to race day. If too many races pay, the list stops being neutral, so cap it at 1–2 per month.

Pick this if

you want to start selling this week to people who already send you races.

What works

Standard sizes (728×90, 300×250, 300×600, 320×100) work with any creative and with programmatic demand, so unsold slots still earn money. They're the easiest to explain to a media buyer.

What risks

The 300×600 needs a new sidebar on articles, which are a single column today. Third-party ads bring cookies and consent requirements, and they slow pages down.

Pick this if

you want unsold inventory backfilled automatically and to sell by CPM.

What works

What risks

There's limited inventory. A month that doesn't sell needs a fallback, either a house ad or a prompt to buy the slot.

Pick this if

you want a few high-value, relationship-based deals per year.

What works

The contextual match is strong: a gel brand on the gel calculator, a watch brand on the pace calculator. The product card uses the tool's own output ("6 τεμ. για το πλάνο σου"), which both buyers and readers like.

What risks

Each tool needs a matching advertiser. Sponsored products inside results can look like the result was bought, so keep the tool's numbers independent of the sponsor.

Pick this if

you have, or want, gel, nutrition and wearable brands as partners.

What works

It's automatically relevant: trail shoes on a mountain race, road racers on a marathon. Race type and distances already exist on every race, so targeting comes free, and it scales to any number of shops.

What risks

It's the heaviest to build (feed ingestion, targeting rules, tracking). Revenue depends on clicks, and the tiles are visually busy on mobile.

Pick this if

you want ongoing, scalable revenue from running shops and are willing to build a feed integration.

How to pick — or stack them

These aren't exclusive. A typical rate card: A and C sold directly, B as programmatic backfill, D and E as partnerships. The existing HouseAd component covers any slot that goes unsold.
